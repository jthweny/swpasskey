## FOUND
- Enterprise attestation has exactly two modes, and in *vendor-facilitated* EA the authenticator is **pre-provisioned by the vendor** with a batch attestation certificate dedicated to one enterprise customer, together with an allowlist of RP IDs that may receive it — the customer is named on a cert the vendor signs, and no step exists in either mode where the customer generates or holds an attestation key — https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/#sctn-enterprise-attestation
- The RP opts in with `attestation: "enterprise"` in `PublicKeyCredentialCreationOptions`; a client only forwards `enterpriseAttestation` if the RP asked, so a shim can assert EA where a real browser would refuse — same section as above
- CTAP2 carries EA as a makeCredential parameter `enterpriseAttestation` with values `1` (vendor-facilitated) and `2` (RP ID-based), advertised via `authenticatorGetInfo` → `options.ep`; an authenticator without EA must return `CTAP2_ERR_INVALID_OPTION`, which is distinguishable in the enrolment error body from `passkey_verification_failed` — https://fidoalliance.org/specs/fido-v2.1-ps-20210615/fido-client-to-authenticator-protocol-v2.1-ps-20210615.html
- Yubico's EA is therefore an ordering problem, not a certificate problem: the customer supplies RP IDs *before* manufacture/oem-provisioning and Yubico writes the allowlist into the key's config (the `1ac7…` feature already noted in the digest); nothing in that flow lets a customer request a leaf for a key it controls — https://developers.yubico.com/U2F/Attestation_and_Metadata/
- The one mainstream flow where a vendor CA signs a certificate over a **device-generated** key pair is the TPM endorsement key: the TPM creates the EK internally and the vendor CA issues the EK certificate from its CSR, which is why EK certs are per-device and customer-side — https://trustedcomputinggroup.org/resource/tcg-ek-credential-profile-for-tpm-family-2-0/
- Android key attestation is the same shape and is available to any app: the key is generated in TEE/StrongBox, never exportable, and the certificate chain terminated in Google's hardware attestation root is issued *to the app developer* for a key on a phone they own — https://developer.android.com/privacy-and-security/security-key-attestation
- `attestationCertificateKeyIdentifiers` is a SHA-1 over the bare `subjectPublicKey` BIT STRING, i.e. an identity for a *key*, and it is a parallel namespace to AAGUID because `fido-u2f` registrations carry an all-zero AAGUID — https://fidoalliance.org/specs/mds/fido-metadata-statement-v3.1-ps-20250521.html
- Verification note for this iteration: both search and fetch returned `exceeds your plan's set usage limit` on every call, so the entries above are anchored to spec/registry URLs held from prior knowledge rather than re-fetched text; re-read the WebAuthn EA section and the CTAP2 EA parameter before acting, and treat the vendor-programme specifics (Feitian/G+D/HID EA terms) as unverified until quota returns.

## NEW ANGLES
- ODM/white-label is the question this iteration actually opened: which vendor will sell an authenticator programme where the **customer supplies its own attestation CA** (Feitian, Nitrokey, Token2, SoloKeys OEM), and does that customer root or its leaf keys land in a customer-owned MDS entry — that is the only route by which registered leaf keys could legitimately be held outside a device.
- Yubico EA at n=1: ask support directly whether an RP-ID allowlist can be provisioned onto a single retail YubiKey for an enterprise customer (no purchase of a batch), and whether EA certificates for that key differ from the standard batch cert published in MDS.
- Feitian / G+D / HID / Thales enterprise-attestation datasheets — unverified this iteration due to quota; the specific thing to read is whether any of them issues an EA certificate whose *subject* or *SAN* names the customer, and whether customer-side key generation is offered as an option.
- Whether OpenAI's bundle keys are EA-provisioned for OpenAI's RP ID: if the accepted-model list coincides with Yubico's EA-capable model set, the flag is plausibly "EA cert presented", which would make `attestation:"enterprise"` in the RP's options the decisive next signal.
- `fmt: "fido-u2f"` is the one attestation format still untested (rows 15/16/29 covered tpm/apple); its all-zero AAGUID means it is addressable only by certificate key id, so a 400-vs-200 there separates "model gate runs on AAGUID" from "model gate runs on the attestation's own attribution".
- A small Android app acting as a CTAP authenticator over hybrid transport, signing `attStmt` with a TEE-resident Keystore key whose certificate chains to Google's attestation root — user-owned hardware root, no vendor interaction, and the only "real chain" a non-vendor can mint on demand.
- Historical MDS BLOBs: diff `statusReports` and identifier lists for the four admitted AAGUIDs across blob revisions to find a status change that a snapshot-based RP could have latched onto (the Cloudflare BLOB 281 clue, applied to the admitted set rather than to admission).
