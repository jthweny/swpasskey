## EXPERIMENTS

1. Enrol `c1f9a0bc` (YubiKey 5 FIPS with NFC, L2 dated 2023-03-28, MDS 2023-03-29) with the existing self-made packed statement; poll signal 1/2 at t+2/10/30 min | expect: 200 + `is_security_key:true` if "L2 or later-MDS-entry", 400 `passkey_verification_failed` if the gate is a model allowlist | proves: whether the gate is certification-level/recency (a *property*, hence generalisable) or a hand-picked list (a *set*, hence only bypassable by claiming a member AAGUID).
2. Enrol `73bb0cd4` (YubiKey 5C FIPS 5.4, L2) and `19083c3d` (YubiKey 5C 5.7, L2, **non-NFC** form factor) | expect: both 200 → gate is not NFC/5C-NFC-specific; 400 on `19083c3d` → gate is form-factor-bound to the exact OpenAI-bundled key | proves: whether the accepted set tracks the *product line* or the *certification/data sheet*.
3. Enrol two non-Yubico L2 models: Token2 PIN+ `eabb46cc-e241-80bf-ae9e-96fa6d2975cf`, and a second L2 model from a different vendor (e.g. Precision InnaIT "FIDO 2 Level 2 certified", find its AAGUID in the MDS explorer) | expect: 200 → the gate is certification-level and vendor-agnostic, opening every L2 vendor's provisioning programme; 400 → allowlist confirmed | proves: vendor-agnosticism of the gate (the brief's core question).
4. Enrol `d7781e5d`, `fmt:"packed"`, with `x5c = [Yubico FIDO Attestation A 1, Yubico Attestation Intermediate A 1, Yubico Attestation Root 1]` (all three PEM strings copied verbatim from the MDS explorer page) and `attStmt.sig` replaced by a signature from a throwaway key | expect: 200 and **flag true** if the verifier path-validates to the MDS-published root set without binding the signature to the leaf (consistent with row 3 proving signatures are unchecked) | proves: the flag is a chain-validation check, i.e. it can be satisfied with **zero private key material** — the first mechanism here that does not require a vendor's attestation key.
5. Chain-truncation bisect on the same AAGUID: `x5c=[Intermediate A 1, Root 1]`; `x5c=[Root 1]`; `x5c=[Yubico FIDO Attestation A 1]` alone; `x5c=[sub-CA, intermediate]` (no root) | expect: flag true only for the variant that terminates at a published root and presents at least one CA:TRUE cert | proves: whether the check requires a *complete* path, tolerates an intermediate-as-leaf, and whether the MDS root list (not the leaf's own issuer) is the anchor set — the exact minimal input needed.
6. Harvest a genuine device leaf and replay it: search GitHub issues/blogs for a posted `attestationObject` from a YubiKey 5C NFC on 5.7.4, CBOR-decode, extract `attStmt.x5c[0]`, submit it as our leaf for AAGUID `d7781e5d` with a junk signature (per-device leaves still chain to the same published sub-CA) | expect: flag true → the check is chain/`attestationCertificateKeyIdentifiers`-based and a *public* certificate is sufficient; flag false → the check binds to something per-device (serial, EA), and the class is closed | proves: whether public attestation material is a usable input, which would make the whole gate reproducible without any vendor relationship.
7. Lazy-computation test: after whichever enrolment in (4)/(6) succeeds, poll signal 1 at t+2/10/30 min **and again immediately after performing one `getAssertion` sign-in with that credential**, and after deleting every other passkey/TOTP so the emulated key is the sole login method ("hardware keys must be the user's only registered login methods") | expect: flag flips only post-assertion or only after credential-pruning | proves: whether classification is computed at enrolment, at first authentication, or by a batch job — and whether `tac1` has a non-credential precondition, which explains the reported delayed updates without invoking rejection.
8. Field bisect on an accepted AAGUID (six enrolments, one variable each): leaf with / without the FIDO AAGUID extension; subject CN = `YubiKey 5 Series with NFC` vs `Yubico FIDO Attestation A 1`; EKU absent vs serverAuth; basicConstraints CA:TRUE vs CA:FALSE; KU = digitalSignature vs certificateSign; validity 9999-12-31 vs 2024-12-01→2027 | expect: at most one or two variants flip the flag | proves: exactly which X.509 attributes the verifier reads, converting the black box into a signable specification.
