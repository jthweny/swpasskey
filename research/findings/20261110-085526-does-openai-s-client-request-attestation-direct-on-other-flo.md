## FOUND

- `attestationObject` (and therefore AAGUID + `attStmt`) exists only in the registration response; the authentication response is `{authenticatorData, clientDataJSON, signature, userHandle}` — there is no field in which a client could convey attestation at login — https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AuthenticatorAssertionResponse
- Normative: "For attestation signatures, the authenticator MUST set the AT flag and include the `attestedCredentialData`. For assertion signatures, the AT flag MUST NOT be set and the `attestedCredentialData` MUST NOT be included." — https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3
- The only per-ceremony data a classifier can read on *every* login is the flags byte, and BE (bit 3, permanent, set at MakeCredential) / BS (bit 4, mutable) are carried in assertion `authenticatorData`; BE=0 ∧ BS=1 is a malformed combination RPs must reject — https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3
- CTAP-side, attestation selection is a MakeCredential-only parameter: `enterpriseAttestation (0x0a)` exists in `authenticatorMakeCredential`, `epAtt (0x04)` only in its result; `authenticatorGetAssertion` has no attestation parameter and no attestation output — https://fidoalliance.org/specs/fido-v2.3-ps-20260226/fido-client-to-authenticator-protocol-v2.3-ps-20260226.html and https://github.com/WebauthnWorks/CTAP2.1-Migration-Guide/blob/main/Options/EnterpriseAttestation.md
- Chromium's conveyance table: `"none"`/absent → empty `none` attestation; `"indirect"`/`"direct"` → *user consent prompt*, then device attestation returned; `"enterprise"` → same plus an "individual attestation" hint to the device. Indirect is currently identical to direct — https://www.chromium.org/security-keys
- Same page, decisive for interpretation of any `fmt:"none"` result: Chrome **replaces attestation with `none` even when direct was requested** for certs with issuers it deems individually identifying (e.g. `FT FIDO 0100`) — https://www.chromium.org/security-keys
- WebAuthn L2 zeroed the AAGUID when conveyance was `none`; L3 removed that zeroing, so a real AAGUID now arrives with `fmt:"none"` and is explicitly *not* proof of attestation — https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-2 vs https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3 (§18.1.1) and https://fidoalliance.org/webauthn-level-3-is-now-a-w3c-recommendation
- `response.getTransports()` is populated by the **client**, is unsigned, and the spec's own guidance ("sites should not interpret this array but instead store it") shows it is advisory metadata, not evidence — https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AuthenticatorAttestationResponse/getTransports
- OpenAI's own eligibility rule is a *server-side approved-model policy*, published only negatively: "Register at least one **approved** physical FIDO2 hardware security key", and "Unsupported FIDO2 hardware security keys — TrustKey T110 and T120 security keys are currently ineligible for individual Daybreak access" — https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001259-troubleshooting-openai-daybreak
- The rule wording has churned in both directions (Yubico's restatement: "Hardware keys must be the user's only registered login methods, and software or synced passkeys do not qualify" vs. the later correction "A second physical key is optional… other passkeys can remain registered") — i.e. `tac1_enabled` is a mutable server-side rule set, not a pure function of the credential — https://www.yubico.com/blog/openai-daybreak-signals-a-new-era-for-ai-security-and-the-critical-role-of-hardware-security-keys and https://community.openai.com/t/trusted-access-for-cyber-issue/1399570?page=2
- Prior art for the "delayed" behaviour: at least one commercial RP re-validates stored registration attestation asynchronously ("Mosaic may re-validate passkeys that provided attestation at registration on a daily basis"), and retroactive AAGUID lists can invalidate already-registered credentials without any login — https://developer.transmitsecurity.com/guides/user/passkey_attestation
- No public capture of OpenAI's `PublicKeyCredentialCreationOptions` (the `attestation` value) exists — searched; community reports describe UI and POST failure codes only — https://community.openai.com/t/daybreak-does-not-recognize-trustkey-t110-physical-fido2-keys-as-hardware-security-keys/1401917

## EXPERIMENTS

1. Intercept the creation-options response for both enrolment surfaces — `chatgpt.com` Settings → Security → *Add passkey*, and `auth.openai.com/advanced-account-security/secure-methods` → *Add a hardware security key* — and diff the JSON | expect: identical `attestation`/`authenticatorSelection` but **different POST endpoint or body field** (e.g. a flow/type discriminator), or `attestation:"direct"` + `authenticatorAttachment:"cross-platform"` only on the AAS surface | proves: whether our nine 200s were even submitted to the surface the classifier listens to — if the endpoints differ, all prior rows are mis-scoped rather than refuted
2. Re-enrol the already-accepted statement (AAGUID `f4ce5fc0`, packed, chain appended) through the **AAS hardware-key endpoint captured in (1)** instead of the generic passkey endpoint, then poll signal 1 at t+2/10/30 min | expect: either flag true (flow-discriminated classification) or false (attestation-discriminated) | proves: separates "endpoint/flow determines the tag" from "attestation determines the tag"; either outcome is a first-class result
3. Vary only the **unsigned client-reported fields** on the same accepted attestation, one at a time: `transports: ["usb"]` → `["usb","nfc"]` → `["internal"]`; `authenticatorAttachment: "cross-platform"` → `"platform"`; `clientExtensionResults.credProps.rk: true/false` | expect: at least one variant flips `is_non_exportable_hardware_key` (most likely `transports` omitting a removable-media transport) | proves: whether the classifier consumes client-declared, unforgeable-by-design-absent metadata — the cheapest untested discriminator left, since all 19 prior rows held these constant
4. Assertion-path reclassification: with an accepted 200 credential, run three real logins spaced 5 min apart carrying BE=0/BS=0, then one with BE=1/BS=1, then one with the malformed BE=0/BS=1, re-querying signal 1 after each | expect: no change on the first three; possible downgrade or error on the malformed one | proves: whether a login-triggered job re-reads flags (would explain "delayed backend state update") or whether classification is registration-only
5. Signature-vs-chain isolation (the untested cell behind row 3): accepted AAGUID + self-signed leaf we hold the key for + `attStmt.sig` **correctly** over `authData‖clientDataHash`, `x5c=[leaf]` only | expect: 200, flag false | proves: the classifier validates sig+chain (not just presence), so a self-issued-but-valid signature is insufficient — isolates "chain to vendor anchor" as the single remaining requirement
6. Verifier X.509 weakness probes on accepted AAGUIDs, all with a signature we can actually produce: (a) `x5c` omitting the leaf, sending only the genuine Yubico intermediate; (b) leaf DN copied from a genuine Yubico leaf but issued by our root, with our root's subject DN set to the genuine Yubico root DN (name-based trust test); (c) cross-signed leaf (our key, two certs, one chaining to a genuine vendor root); (d) pathLenConstraint=0 on genuine intermediate with our leaf beneath it | expect: all false/400, but (a) or (b) could return 200 *with* flag true if trust is name- or index-based | proves: whether any accepted path skips signature verification of the leaf→intermediate step, which would be a directly usable bypass
7. `tac1_enabled` dependency control: on a fixed credential set, query `POST /backend-api/cyber_verification/status` (a) after adding a second credential, (b) after removing all non-accepted credentials, (c) after identity verification completes, (d) 24 h later with no changes | expect: `tac1_enabled` tracks identity + credential-set composition, not the flag alone | proves: whether signal 2 is a separate conjunct (answering "does classification unlock Daybreak") or merely mirrors signal 1

## NEW ANGLES

- Client-declared `transports` / `authenticatorAttachment` / `credProps` are the only inputs in the registration POST that are unsigned *and* client-chosen; a classifier that couldn't anchor attestation may have fallen back to them — the highest-value unexplored variable set.
- The AAS enrolment lives on `auth.openai.com`, a different origin (and possibly different RP ID) from `chatgpt.com` — map both origins' credential lists and enrolment endpoints; a credential enrolled at one origin may not be the record the Daybreak check reads.
- Chrome's direct-attestation **consent prompt** is a free side channel for whether `attestation:"direct"` was actually requested on a given surface — observe it once, no proxy needed.
- Enterprise attestation path: advertise `ep` in `GetInfo`, return `epAtt:true` with enterprise packed; combined with `chrome://flags/#web-authentication-permit-enterprise-attestation` this is the one CTAP branch where the device's own attestation claims can differ in kind, not just in chain.
- Isolate the classifier from the enrolment verifier by feeding it a *known-good* credential record (real YubiKey enrolment on a throwaway account, capture the exact POST body) then mutating exactly one field at a time towards our emulated material — binary-searches the discriminator instead of guessing.
- Check whether OpenAI trusts AAGUID alone under L3's "AAGUID without attestation" allowance by submitting `fmt:"none"` with each accepted AAGUID plus a non-zero AAGUID and a plausible `transports` set (row 12 held AAGUID constant while removing attestation; the reverse cell is open).
- Queue for later iterations: FIDO certified-but-software authenticators and MDS BLOB entry cost (avenue 5), and vendors that issue attestation leaves to customers (Feitian/G+D/HID/Token2/Nitrokey) — still the only route that yields a genuine chain without buying the exact bundle model.
