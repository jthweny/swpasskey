## EXPERIMENTS
1. **Anchor-by-name spoof.** For accepted AAGUID `a25342c0`, mint a self-signed CA whose subject DN, `serialNumber`, `SKI`/`AKI` and validity byte-copy `Yubico U2F Root CA Serial 457200631` (PEM is in the MDS entry), then issue a leaf from *our* key with the AAGUID ext, submit `fmt: packed`, poll signals 1–2 at t+2/10/30. | expect: `is_non_exportable_hardware_key: false` | proves: the flag check compares anchors cryptographically (signature/path), not by DN/SKI/serial — and if it flips, the whole anchor problem is solved for all four accepted models at once.
2. **Genuine batch leaf + junk signature.** Set `x5c[0]` to a *real* Yubico 5.7.4 batch attestation cert (public half; obtainable from any physical 5C NFC fw 5.7.4 via `ykman`/attestation dump, or from any published `attestationObject` hex dump), `x5c[1..]` to `Yubico FIDO Attestation A 1` + `Attestation Intermediate B 1` + `Root 1` from the MDS entry, and a random 64‑byte `sig`. | expect: one of two clean outcomes — flag `true` ⇒ verification is chain‑only and a borrowed public batch cert suffices (no vendor key needed); flag `false` ⇒ `sig` is verified and only key possession works | proves: which of the two remaining mechanisms the flag actually is; this is the single highest‑information test left.
3. **Exhaustive gate sweep with zero attestation.** Decode the MDS3 BLOB, take every `protocolFamily: fido2` AAGUID (~500), and POST each to `passkey_verification` with `fmt: "none"`, empty `attStmt` (proven admitted for accepted models, row 12), recording 200/400. | expect: 200 for exactly the four known AAGUIDs plus maybe a handful of siblings | proves: the accepted set is finite and enumerable; combined with experiment 4 it answers this iteration's question definitively instead of by sampling.
4. **Anchor-type audit of the accepted set.** For each AAGUID that returned 200 in (3), parse its `attestationRootCertificates` and print `basicConstraints`, self-signed-ness, `keyUsage`, and whether the leaf's issuer matches an anchor directly. | expect: every accepted entry has ≥1 CA anchor issued by a vendor HSM CA; zero end‑entity anchors | proves: "material a non-vendor can hold" does not exist on the accepted list, so the remaining paths are exactly (leaked key | verifier weakness | genuine key).
5. **`fido-u2f` format probe.** Re-submit `a25342c0` with `fmt: "fido-u2f"` (its MDS bag explicitly trusts the U2F root) and a DER ECDSA `sig`, `x5c` = our self-signed leaf. | expect: 400 or 200-with-false; anything other than the packed path's response is informative | proves: whether the admitted-format set is exactly `{packed}` per AAGUID family or globally, and whether the U2F branch reaches different (possibly weaker) chain logic.
6. **Dual-oracle divergence check.** After each enrolment above, record `is_non_exportable_hardware_key` **and** the eligibility payload's `qualifying_count`/`non_qualifying_count` at t+2/10/30. | expect: the two move together | proves: whether signal 1 is the binding constraint at all, or whether Daybreak keys off a different field we have never read.
7. **MDS-vintage / expired-anchor probe.** Enrol `34f5766d-1536-4a24-9033-0e294e510fb0` ("YubiKey 5 Series with NFC Preview") whose anchors are `Yubico FIDO Preview CA 2020` (expired 2021‑12‑31) and `Yubico 2023 FIDO Preview CA` (expired 2024‑12‑31), with a leaf claiming to chain to the expired one. | expect: 400 (model gate) or 200 + flag false (expiry enforced) | proves: if 200, the verifier's path validation ignores `notAfter` on anchors — a reusable hole for any future material.
8. **Published-key grep.** Compute the SHA‑256 of every cert in the FIDO conformance EA test material (`FIDO2 Enterprise Attestation TEST ROOT` and the batch key) and grep the decoded MDS BLOB for a matching anchor or SPKI. | expect: no production match | proves: whether any vendor ever adopted a public‑key test root; if yes, we hold a working attestation key today.
9. **Transport-hint probe.** Re-enrol an accepted AAGUID while forcing the browser bridge to report `getTransports() = ["usb"]` vs `["hybrid"]`/`["internal"]`. | expect: no change to the flag, possible change to the UI label ("Passkey" vs "security key") | proves: whether OpenAI's classifier consumes browser-reported transport (as the TrustKey "Passkey" label hints) rather than attestation — a completely different and much cheaper lever than attestation.

## NEW ANGLES
- **Enterprise attestation as a separate gate.** YubiKeys on EA‑capable firmware produce per‑device EA certs with the serial in the DN, and FIDO publishes EA test material including private keys. Check whether OpenAI requests `attestation: "enterprise"` and whether the flag keys off `getClientExtensionResults().enterpriseAttestation` rather than `packed` chain validity — an EA‑only requirement would explain why every packed chain we mint is uniformly `false` regardless of anchor.
- **RP trust-store vintage as an attack surface.** Cloudflare's case shows RPs pin/sync a specific MDS BLOB and reject chains whose root arrived in a later blob (Yubico 5.7.x = BLOB 127). Determine which BLOB OpenAI's verifier is pinned to by submitting leaves chaining to anchors introduced in different blobs (`Yubico FIDO Root CA Serial 450203556`, `U2F Root CA 457200631`, `Attestation Root 1`) and see which vintages are even reachable.
- **OEM/attestation‑provisioning programmes** where the *customer* holds the attestation key: NXP EdgeLock SE05x and Infineon OPTIGA provisioning services, Token2 programmable tokens, and Feitian's customisation line — the question to ask each vendor is narrow and answerable: "will you sign a customer‑supplied leaf under your MDS‑listed root, and will you list the resulting AAGUID."
- **MDS portal economics for our own AAGUID** (FIDO membership tier, L1 vs L2, BLOB signing, and what `keyProtection` a software authenticator is permitted to claim) — read the MDS Portal user guides and the certification policy, then price it.
- **Community-sourced negative list.** Since OpenAI publishes only exclusions (TrustKey T110/T120), mine the Daybreak/Yubico/Reddit/Discord threads for every model reported as *working* vs *labelled Passkey* to build the allowlist empirically before spending a single request.
