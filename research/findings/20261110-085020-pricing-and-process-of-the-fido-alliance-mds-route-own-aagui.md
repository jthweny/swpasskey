## FOUND

- Membership dues (as of Jan 2026): Associate **$3,250** (≤100 employees) / **$18,000** (101+), Sponsor **$30,250**, Board **$60,500**, Government **$18,000** — https://fidoalliance.org/members/become-a-member
- Authenticator certification fees: Functional **$6,000** member / **$9,000** non-member; **L1 security included at no extra fee**; L1+ and higher **$9,000 / $13,500**; derivative $1,000/$1,500; delta $1,200/$1,800 — https://fidoalliance.org/fido-certification-fees
- L1 is explicitly open to software: "L1 | Any device, software or hardware", assessed by FIDO Security Secretariat **document review, no lab** — https://fidoalliance.org/security-certification-authenticator-security-levels ; same table in the requirements doc ("L1 | Any device HW or SW") — https://fidoalliance.org/specs/fido-security-requirements/fido-authenticator-security-requirements-v1.5-fd-20211102.html
- L1+ is the software-hardening tier (whitebox crypto, anti-tamper, lab vulnerability testing); **L2 requires execution inside a hardware AROE** — a pure-software authenticator is structurally excluded from L2, not merely priced out — https://fidoalliance.org/security-certification-authenticator-security-levels
- **MDS listing does not require certification**: "If you do not have a certified authenticator and do not plan to certify your authenticator, you can skip to the section on metadata management. FIDO Certification is an optional service and is not required for listing your metadata statement." — https://fidoalliance.org/mds-portal-user-guide-for-vendor-users
- MDS Portal registration is self-service (corporate email domain required; first user self-registers as Vendor Admin, later users invited) — https://fidoalliance.org/mds-portal-user-guide-for-vendor-admins
- No MDS submission fee appears anywhere in the submission terms or the fee schedule; the only published money is certification, and metadata submission is listed as an **optional post-certification step** ("Metadata Submission to MDS (Optional)") — https://fidoalliance.org/metadata-submission-terms-for-authenticator-vendors-2 , https://fidoalliance.org/certification/authenticator-certification-levels
- **BLOB signing is not a vendor step and costs nothing**: FIDO signs the single MDS3 BLOB with a chain anchored at the GlobalSign R46 root; vendors only supply statements/fields, RPs verify against R46 — https://fidoalliance.org/metadata , https://docs.nevis.net/configurationguide/nevis-fido2/fido2-concept-and-integration-guide/configuration/fido2-authenticator-metadata
- `keyProtection` is a **required** metadata field and must describe *effective* security: "if a key is stored in a secure element but software running on the FIDO User Device could call a function in the secure element to export the key … then the effective security is `KEY_PROTECTION_SOFTWARE`"; `software` is mutually exclusive with `hardware`/`tee`/`secure_element` — https://fidoalliance.org/specs/common-specs/fido-registry-v2.2-rd-20210525.html , https://fidoalliance.org/specs/mds/fido-metadata-statement-v3.1-ps-20250521.html
- Trust anchor may be anything: "A trust anchor can be a root certificate, an intermediate CA certificate or **even the attestation certificate itself**" — so a self-signed leaf is a *legitimate* declared anchor, not a malformed statement (this is the spec basis for the Securitag/end-entity-anchor class) — https://fidoalliance.org/specs/mds/fido-metadata-statement-v3.1-ps-20250521.html
- MDS carries self-asserted, uncertified entries by design: statuses include `NOT_FIDO_CERTIFIED` and `SELF_ASSERTION_SUBMITTED`, and MDS metadata is "for informational purposes only … do not guarantee the accuracy or completeness" — https://fidoalliance.org/specs/mds/fido-metadata-service-v3.1-ps-20250521.html , https://fidoalliance.org/metadata-usage-terms-for-relying-parties-or-service-providers-2
- FIDO states it does **not** verify `certifications` in authenticatorGetInfo; only `StatusReport` is authoritative — https://fidoalliance.org/specs/mds/fido-metadata-statement-v3.1-ps-20250521.html
- MDS admins check that the identifier belongs to the submitter's company ("The AAID for which the statement was submitted belongs to the submitter's company"), i.e. AAGUID ownership is validated at ingestion — https://fidoalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/FIDO_Alliance_Metadata_Service_White_Paper_02122016.pdf (also: submitted metadata is "verified to be an accurate representation of the implementation" during certification — https://fidoalliance.org/specs/fido-security-requirements/fido-authenticator-metadata-requirements-v1.5.1-fd-20251016.pdf)
- FIDO publishes **test attestation certificates with their private keys** (FIDO2 Enterprise Attestation test leaf, anchored at "FIDO2 Enterprise Attestation TEST ROOT", RPIDs `enterprisetest.certinfra.fidoalliance.org`) — https://github.com/fido-alliance/conformance-test-tools-resources/blob/main/docs/FIDO2/Authenticator/README.md
- Deployment-side truth about the target: OpenAI AAS needs two secure sign-in methods incl. one cross-device; "any FIDO-compatible security key" is claimed to work — https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001221-advanced-account-security ; but for Daybreak individuals, "software or synced passkeys do not qualify", hardware keys must be the only registered login methods, and the check runs **before identity verification** — https://www.yubico.com/blog/openai-daybreak-signals-a-new-era-for-ai-security-and-the-critical-role-of-hardware-security-keys
- Inference from rows 1–9 + the above: the accepted Yubico 5C/Titan entries already declare `keyProtection: ["hardware","secure_element"]` in MDS, yet our credentials on those AAGUIDs are `is_non_exportable_hardware_key: false`. The flag is therefore **not** a read of MDS `keyProtection`; it requires something validated per-credential that we have not yet produced. Also: an *honest* software authenticator can only ever publish `["software"]`, so the MDS route can at best open the model gate, never self-declare hardware.

## EXPERIMENTS

1. Clean discriminator for the 400s: enrol `5df66f62` (Securitag) and `bb878d7b` (TruU) with `fmt: "none"`, empty attStmt, zero-length AAGUID-claim work — no certificate at all. Run each 3× | expect: **400** if the gate is an AAGUID allowlist; **200 + flag false** if the gate is attestation-chain validation (row 12 already shows `fmt:"none"` passes for an accepted model) | proves: whether the 400 class means "model not admitted" or "chain not valid" — this is the single measurement that decides if "get a real vendor chain" is even the right axis.
2. MDS-driven acceptance sweep: `curl https://mds.fidoalliance.org/` → verify JWT against GlobalSign R46 → collect every `aaguid` (dedupe, FIDO2 only). Enrol each with one fixed packed self-signed leaf + AAGUID extension, 1 req/s, record status + body, abort on 3 consecutive 429s | expect: exactly `f4ce5fc0`, `d7781e5d`, `a25342c0`, `42b4fb4a` return 200; every other Entry returns 400 | proves: the admitted-on-sight set is the complete allowlist and is not MDS-wide (200s are unambiguous; 400s are ambiguous by design, which is why experiment 1 runs first). Delete created credentials afterwards.
3. Same BLOB, offline forensics: for each entry dump `keyProtection`, `attestationTypes`, `attestationRootCertificates` count, `statusReports`; then (a) diff `f4ce5fc0` vs `2fc0579f` vs `f8a011f3` field-by-field, (b) list entries where `keyProtection == ["software"]`, (c) list entries whose anchors are byte-identical to the FIDO EP test root / "Sample Attestation Root" / Chromium test roots, (d) list all AAGUIDs whose description matches `IDmelon|Allthenticate|Yubico|Titan` | expect: (a) accepted and refused same-vendor entries are indistinguishable on `keyProtection`/`attestationTypes`; (b) at least one software-keyProtection entry exists; (c) zero entries anchored to public-key test roots | proves: (a) the gate cannot be metadata-derived, (b) software models do get listed, (c) no "free private key" entry exists to claim — and (d) feeds experiment 4.
4. IDmelon/Allthenticate AAGUID exhaust: for every AAGUID from 3(d) — not just `39a5647e` — enrol with our self-signed packed leaf | expect: 400 on all; if any 200, our row-19 AAGUID was simply the wrong variant | proves: whether the "phone app that qualifies" data point is real, and whether accepted-on-sight covers non-hardware app authenticators (which would make the model gate and the flag independent).
5. Chain-signature probe on an accepted model: enrol `f4ce5fc0` with `x5c = [our leaf (issuer DN = "CN=Yubico FIDO Attestation B 1", signed by our own key), genuine Yubico intermediate, genuine Yubico root]`, real signature over authData‖clientDataHash | expect: 200 (enrolment does not validate X.509 signatures per row 4), then poll signals 1–2 at t+2/10/30 min → still false | proves: enrolment and flag-setting are separate passes; the async pass does the real path validation. A flip to true would mean path building only matches issuer names — jackpot.
6. Anchor-as-leaf edge case on an accepted model: `x5c = [genuine Yubico attestation root cert alone]`, attStmt.sig 64 zero bytes | expect: 200, flag false at t+30 | proves: whether the verifier accepts a trust anchor presented as the end entity (the `attestationRootCertificates` spec allows it, so a lenient verifier would). Also try `x5c=[our self-signed root, genuine Yubico root]` with our leaf's `basicConstraints=CA:TRUE` as leaf.
7. Client-side allowlist recon before any registry work: fetch `chatgpt.com` HTML + all referenced JS chunks (and the auth subdomain's chunks), grep for `is_non_exportable_hardware_key`, `tac1_enabled`, `passkey_verification_failed`, and any 36-char UUIDs; also string-match the error payload to identify the identity vendor (Auth0/WorkOS/Stytch/Descope) and then read that vendor's published attestation-policy docs | expect: either a literal AAGUID array (the gate, verbatim) or a vendor SDK name | proves: the mechanism without spending $9,250, and tells us whose docs describe the classification rule.
8. Harmless MDS ingestion dry-run: register MDS Portal with a corporate domain, submit metadata for a **fresh own AAGUID**, `keyProtection: ["software"]`, `attestationRootCertificates: [our own leaf]`, status `NOT_FIDO_CERTIFIED`, anchor = our leaf (spec-legal). Then enrol that AAGUID with our leaf and poll 1–2 for 30 min | expect: 400 at enrolment both before and after the BLOB refreshes | proves: OpenAI's trust store is not a live MDS pull. (Do **not** submit metadata under a Yubico AAGUID to test the same thing — FIDO validates identifier ownership at ingestion, and a forged statement would poison every MDS-consuming RP, not just this account.)

## NEW ANGLES

- Error-string attribution: `passkey_verification_failed` and `tac1_enabled` are idiosyncratic; identifying the IdP product behind them turns an opaque allowlist into documented policy (many IdPs publish "hardware-backed attestation" allowlists and MDS-refresh cadence).
- White-label/ODM roots: several MDS entries share anchors across reseller brands (Feitian, Hypersecu, Excelsecu, SecuGen builds). If any such AAGUID is on the accepted list, a reseller-branded leaf may be obtainable commercially — cheaper than certification.
- September 2026 MDS changelog added listable fields for Authenticator Security Requirements 1.7 and Authenticator Policy 1.4.1 — check whether new per-entry fields (policy version, `isKeyRestricted`, `operatingEnv`) landed in the BLOB, since those are exactly the discriminators a verifier would need and may be what the flag actually reads.
- Enterprise Attestation as the legitimate "customer holds a vendor-signed leaf" programme: verify whether any vendor (Yubico EA, Feitian, G+D) issues per-organization attestation certs whose *key* or issuance the customer controls, versus merely per-org batch certs held by the vendor.
- `attestationCertificateKeyIdentifiers`-only entries (U2F-style, no AAGUID): a verifier that matches by certificate SKI instead of AAGUID has a different acceptance surface than the one we have been probing.
