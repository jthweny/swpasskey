## FOUND
- Token2 provisions the AAGUID itself, on its own line: "Token2's role on these products is to load our own FIDO2 (PIN+) applet and provision each device: firmware-level PIN complexity, the Token2 AAGUID, and our USB VID/PID are applied on our provisioning line in Geneva." No customer-supplied-AAGUID SKU is mentioned anywhere on the page — https://www.token2.com/pages/fido2-security-keys-faq
- Token2 does issue *per-key* attestation certificates, not one shared batch cert: "Each Token2 key contains a unique attestation certificate issued by Token2's Certificate Authority (CA). This certificate is embedded in the key and also stored in the FIDO Metadata Service (MDS)" — https://www.token2.com/pages/how-to-verify-your-token2-fido2-key
- Token2 PIN+ MDS entry (`eabb46cce24180bfae9e96fa6d2975cf`): FIDO_CERTIFIED_L2, keyProtection "Hardware, Secure Element", attestation "Basic Full", and its **published attestation root is a self-signed end-entity cert** (`Subject: token2.com`, `Issuer: token2.com (self-signed root)`, Explorer labels it `Type: End-entity`, 2019→2072) — https://www.token2.com/tools/fido2-mds/eabb46cce24180bfae9e96fa6d2975cf
- That end-entity-anchor shape is exactly the class that returned 400 in rows 10–11 (Securitag `5df66f62`), so Token2 is a *certified, L2, hardware-protected* model that sits inside the refused anchor class rather than outside it.
- Token2's second, older AAGUID is `ab32f0c62239afbbc470d2ef4e254db7` ("TOKEN2 FIDO2 Security Key", CTAP `certifications: FIDO 1`), reported straight off a T2F2 device — https://github.com/keepassxreboot/keepassxc/discussions/9506 ; listed by Microsoft as TOKEN2 FIDO2 Security Key — https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/authentication/concept-fido2-hardware-vendor
- Token2's Java Card FIDO2 applet source is public and buildable on JCOP3/J3H145-class cards, i.e. the firmware side is not the obstacle; the attestation key/cert is the obstacle — https://github.com/token2/pin_plus_firmware
- **MDS listing does not require certification and does not require FIDO membership**: "FIDO Certification is an optional service and is not required for listing your metadata statement"; publishing a statement is self-service plus MDS Admin approval — https://fidoalliance.org/mds-portal-user-guide-for-vendor-users
- MDS submission terms bind only the uploader (liability capped at $250) and require spec-compliant statements, not certification — https://fidoalliance.org/metadata-submission-terms-for-authenticator-vendors-2
- Cost ladder if certification *is* wanted: authenticator functional cert $6,000 member / $9,000 non-member, L1 included, L1+ and L2 $9,000 / $13,500 — https://fidoalliance.org/fido-certification-fees ; membership dues $3,250–$60,500 — https://fidoalliance.org/members/become-a-member
- Attestation is defined as per-model batch material ("attestation private key is shared between a batch of at least 100,000 security keys of the same model") and OpenSK lets a builder substitute their own cert/key — https://github.com/google/OpenSK/blob/develop/docs/customization.md
- **A real, physical, attested non-bundle key is still rejected by OpenAI**: TrustKey T110/T120 enrol fine and are still shown as "Passkey", Continue disabled — https://community.openai.com/t/daybreak-does-not-recognize-trustkey-t110-physical-fido2-keys-as-hardware-security-keys/1401917
- OpenAI's own troubleshooting page now names them: "Unsupported FIDO2 hardware security keys — TrustKey T110 and T120 security keys are currently ineligible for individual Daybreak access" — https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001259-troubleshooting-openai-daybreak
- OpenAI's support position, as relayed by those users: only keys from the OpenAI/Yubico bundle are confirmed supported; the official docs never enumerate the supported set, only "at least one compatible FIDO2 hardware security key" — https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001258-openai-daybreak-trusted-access-for-cyber-overview
- The bundle's SKUs are YubiKey C NFC and YubiKey C Nano, which is the only public hint at the allowlist's contents — https://www.yubico.com/blog/openai-daybreak-signals-a-new-era-for-ai-security-and-the-critical-role-of-hardware-security-keys
- Requirement text conflicts between sources: Yubico says hardware keys must be the user's *only* login methods, OpenAI's help center says other passkeys may stay — i.e. prose is not a spec; only the API/UI signals are.
- Delayed/asynchronous classification is reported in the wild: after re-login "I didn't make any further changes, but when I checked `/cyber` again later, it had updated itself to the completed state … 'You're all set'" — https://community.openai.com/t/trusted-access-for-cyber-issue/1399570?page=2 ; OpenAI support also attributed misclassification of newly added keys to an active incident, same thread.
- Third-party guidance on the phone-app route explicitly hedges: "Daybreak specifically asks for a verified hardware security key. IDmelon's FIDO2 support does not guarantee acceptance" — https://marduc812.com/2026/10/06/chatgpt-daybreak-idmelon-phone-security-key

## EXPERIMENTS
1. Ask Token2, in four numbered questions, from support@token2.com (cc the sales address on their FIDO Alliance listing, +41 22 519 62 02): (a) will you provision a customer-supplied AAGUID onto PIN+ hardware; (b) will you sign a customer-supplied attestation sub-CA or a CSR, so the leaf chains to `token2.com`; (c) is the resulting model listable in MDS under the customer's name; (d) MOQ and price per unit. Attach the observation that their MDS anchor is an end-entity cert, and ask whether that is required or incidental. | expect: logo-only co-branding offered, AAGUID/sub-CA refused, no published SKU | proves: whether "attestation as a service" exists as a purchasable product at the one vendor whose firmware we can already emulate, and whether the end-entity anchor is a deliberate constraint
2. Claim Token2 AAGUID `eabb46cc-e241-80bf-ae9e-96fa6d2975cf` with our own self-signed leaf (no chain), enrol, then re-poll `passkeys` + `cyber_verification/status` at t+2/10/30 min. | expect: 400 `passkey_verification_failed` | proves: whether the gate is bundle-only (400 for every non-bundle model regardless of cert) or MDS-derived (would admit a certified L2 hardware model)
3. Repeat 2 with `ab32f0c62239afbbc470d2ef4e254db7` (same vendor, older uncertified key) to separate "certified model" from "known model". | expect: 400 | proves: certification level is not what the gate reads
4. Token2 chain-shape probe: build a leaf with issuer `CN=token2.com, O=TOKEN2, C=CH` and append Token2's real published root PEM verbatim from their MDS page as `x5c[1]`; enrol under `eabb46cc`. | expect: 200 admission with flag false, or 400 if the anchor's missing CA flag fails the chain validator | proves: whether an end-entity anchor is rejected on shape (rows 10–11) rather than on model, which would also disqualify genuine Token2 hardware
5. Anchor-flag discriminator inside an *admitted* model: take `f4ce5fc0-…` (Yubico 5C NFC, 200 today) and submit the same credential twice — once with a CA:FALSE self-signed anchor carrying the AAGUID extension, once with a proper CA:TRUE anchor. Compare `is_non_exportable_hardware_key` across both. | expect: false in both | proves: the flag is not a function of chain shape at all, isolating it to (model × chain-validity)
6. Bundle-SKU mapping: read Yubico's published AAGUID table, extract the AAGUIDs for exactly the two bundle SKUs (YubiKey C NFC, YubiKey C Nano), and enrol each distinct AAGUID. | expect: 200 for both; 400 for the Security Key / 5.2-firmware entries already tested | proves: the allowlist is SKU-level and derivable from the bundle, giving a concrete enumeration method instead of guessing
7. Ordering/interference test: with one admitted (200, flag false) credential already on the account, add a second credential with the same admitted AAGUID but `fmt: "none"`, then re-check whether the *first* credential's flag flips. | expect: no change | proves: classification is per-credential and immutable after enrolment (if it *does* flip, classification is recomputed over the credential set, which is a new lever)
8. Re-auth-triggered recompute: pick any credential that returned 200/false, sign out fully, sign in using that credential in a fresh profile, then poll `passkeys` and `status`. | expect: flag may flip true without any new attestation | proves: whether the flag is written by an async worker at assertion time — the community "it updated itself later" case — which would mean the correct experiment loop is credential *use*, not re-enrolment
9. MDS self-listing dry run: register an MDS Portal vendor account, publish a metadata statement for a fresh AAGUID with our own root, `keyProtection: hardware`, `attestationTypes: basic_full`, then enrol that AAGUID. | expect: statement approved without certification within days; enrolment 400 | proves: whether OpenAI consumes MDS at request time (would flip admission) or pins its own list (no effect) — and closes avenue 5 of the brief for ~$0
10. TrustKey control: enrol `TrustKey T110` AAGUID with our own cert. | expect: 400 | proves: reproduces the community's real-hardware false in our harness, calibrating that our emulator's 400/200 boundary matches physical-device behaviour
11. Google Titan v2 with a *CA-flagged* anchor and full extension set, re-run after the row 7 result, polling at t+30. | expect: 200, flag false | proves: Titan is admitted but not hardware-listed, i.e. admission is bundle-derived while the hardware flag needs a second, stricter condition

## NEW ANGLES
- Classify on credential *use*, not just enrolment: the async-worker hypothesis means a "failure" recorded at t+30 may still be pending. Instrument every assertion (getAssertion) and poll the two endpoints after each one, per credential, in a table.
- Enumerate the allowlist from procurement, not from MDS: OpenAI/Yubico bundle SKUs, the Titan v2 partnership, and any other vendor OpenAI publicly thanks. One AAGUID probe per SKU turns partner announcements into a testable list.
- Enterprise attestation as the only per-device Yubico cert surface: check whether an EA-capable YubiKey with a vendor-imprinted RP ID list produces a different classification, and whether EA statements are accepted at all by this endpoint (nothing in the measured rows touches EA).
- Remote/WebAuthn-as-a-service providers that hold real allowlisted hardware behind an API: if any exists that will perform a registration ceremony for an arbitrary RP ID on a YubiKey 5C NFC, the credential is created on genuine silicon without us owning the key. Worth a targeted search, not a purchase.
- Ask TrustKey directly what changed and when: a vendor that was de-listed knows the list's shape, and their answer is cheaper than another 34 AAGUID probes.
- Re-test the "second key" question against the API rather than the prose: does a second credential of the same admitted AAGUID with `fmt:"none"` change anything, and does deleting another passkey change anything — the requirement text is contradictory, so treat key-count as an unmeasured variable.
